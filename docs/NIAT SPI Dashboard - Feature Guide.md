# NIAT SPI Dashboard — Feature Guide

2026-09-17 · prepared by @Ankush

## Overview & who uses it

The SPI (Skill Performance Index) Dashboard exists to answer one recurring question for NIAT's partner colleges: which students are falling behind on attendance or quiz completion, and what needs to happen next to bring them back on track. Before this tool, that answer lived scattered across raw BigQuery tables that only a handful of technical people could query directly. The dashboard turns that same underlying data into role-appropriate screens that a non-technical campus coordinator, a BOA reviewing correction requests, or a recovery instructor reporting on a class they just ran can all use without ever touching SQL.

Underneath, the app draws on two very different kinds of data. Attendance percentages, quiz completion, and session-level detail are computed live from BigQuery — a large, read-only analytics warehouse fed by NxtWave's core systems, holding the full historical record for every student at every partner campus. The dashboard's own operational state — who has a login, what corrections a student has requested, which recovery classes have been scheduled or completed — lives in a separate, much smaller database that belongs to this app alone. Practically, this means the attendance number you see for a student is always a live read of the warehouse (it updates as soon as upstream systems record new sessions), while the recovery status you see is this app's own record of what staff have actually done about it.

Six roles share the same app, and the entire experience — which sidebar items show up, which students and campuses are visible, which buttons even render — reshapes itself around whichever one is signed in:

- **Super Admin** — unrestricted access to every campus, every student, and every page, including the Data Explorer (a raw browser over the underlying BigQuery tables that no other role can reach).
- **Admin** — the same full data access as Super Admin across every campus and student, plus the ability to manage staff accounts and the campus list, but without the Data Explorer.
- **HOD** (Head of Department) — read access to all data across all campuses, matching what an Admin sees, but without any of the admin management tools (no User Access, no Campus Setup) — a pure oversight role.
- **Capability Manager** — sees only the campuses and subjects explicitly assigned to their account. This is enforced at the API layer, not just hidden in the UI: requesting data outside an assigned scope simply returns nothing.
- **BOA** (Branch of Academics) — scoped to their own assigned campus (or campuses), similarly enforced server-side; this is also the role most directly responsible for reviewing and deciding attendance-correction requests from students.
- **Instructor** — the most different experience of the six. Instead of landing on the shared Dashboard, an instructor is routed straight to a dedicated "Report Recovery" page after login, and their sidebar shrinks down to just that page plus the Recovery tab — reflecting that an instructor's job in this system is narrowly to run and report on recovery classes, not to browse analytics.

One detail worth understanding early, because it shapes how every other section behaves: a user's scope (which campuses and subjects they can see) is not something re-checked from a live database lookup on every click — it is baked into that user's session at the moment they log in, and stays fixed until they log in again. If an admin changes someone's assigned campuses while that person is still logged in, the change won't take effect for them until their next login.

## Dashboard

The Dashboard is the first screen every non-instructor role lands on after signing in. It is a single page component, but it is not a single fixed layout — the same page reads the signed-in user's role and scope and assembles a different mix of tiles, charts, and shortcuts for each. An Admin sees campus-wide numbers with a "Manage Users" shortcut in the header; a BOA sees only their own campus's numbers with a "Request Inbox" shortcut instead; a Capability Manager sees the subset of subjects they've been assigned. Nobody is looking at a generic dashboard with irrelevant buttons greyed out — each role's version only contains what's relevant to them.

The building blocks that make up the page:

- **Header quick actions** — one or two buttons tailored to the role, so the next likely action is always one click away rather than buried in a sidebar (Manage Users for admins, Request Inbox for BOA/HOD, and so on).
- **KPI tiles** — a compact strip of headline numbers: total students in scope, number of campuses, an overall average attendance percentage, a count of subjects currently below the 80% attendance threshold, how many sections/subjects are being tracked in total, and average quiz completion. These numbers are cached for a short period (about a minute) rather than recomputed on every page view, so there can be a brief lag between something changing in the underlying data and it showing up here.
- **"Attendance by Subject" chart** — a visual comparison across subjects, making it easy to spot which subjects are dragging the average down at a glance rather than reading through a table.
- **"Subject Health" donut** — subjects bucketed into Healthy, Recovery, and Critical bands purely by attendance percentage, giving a one-look sense of how many subjects are in trouble campus- or institute-wide.
- **Leaderboards and watch-lists** — "Needs Attention" (the students with the lowest attendance in scope), "Campus Breakdown", "Subject Performance", and "Sections to Watch". Each of these is deliberately not a dead-end list: every row links straight into the relevant detail page (a student into their record, a subject into Student Attendance Stats, a campus into Campus Analytics), so the Dashboard functions as a jumping-off point rather than a page you have to leave and re-navigate from.
- **Pending-request banner** — shown only to roles that can act on attendance-correction requests (Admin, Super Admin, BOA, HOD), this banner surfaces whenever there are unreviewed correction requests waiting, so that queue doesn't silently build up unnoticed.

## Student Directory

The Student Directory is the master, searchable list of every student the signed-in user's role and scope entitle them to see — for a Super Admin or Admin that's every student across every partner campus; for a BOA or Capability Manager it's automatically narrowed to their assigned campus(es) before the page even renders. Think of it as the entry point for "find one specific student and see where they stand" as opposed to the Dashboard, which is built for "see the aggregate picture."

- **Search and sort** — free-text search matches on name, student ID, or the underlying user ID, so staff can find a student even if they only have a partial ID from a message or a spreadsheet. Results can be sorted by attendance, putting the students who most need attention at the top or bottom depending on how it's sorted.
- **Filters** — campus (hidden entirely for BOA, since their view is already locked to their own campus — there's no point showing a filter with only one possible value), section, an attendance band filter with four options (All / Below 50% / Below 80% — the Recovery band / 80%+), and separate bands for classroom-quiz and module-quiz completion. Combining filters lets staff answer compound questions directly — e.g. "show me every student in Section B who is both below 80% attendance and below 50% on module quizzes" — without exporting data and filtering it manually elsewhere.
- **The table itself** — Name, Student ID, Campus, Section, Attendance %, Classroom Quiz %, Module Quiz %, and a direct link out to that student's own public SPI report (the same page the student themselves would see if they opened their link, letting staff verify exactly what the student is looking at).
- **Export and scale** — a CSV export button pulls the current filtered view (capped at the first 5,000 matching rows — a query that matches more than that will silently only export the first 5,000, with no on-screen warning that rows were left out), and the table itself is paginated so large campuses don't have to load every row into the browser at once.

## Student Attendance Stats

Student Attendance Stats rolls attendance up to the subject level rather than the individual student level — the question this page answers is "which subjects, campus-wide, have an attendance problem," with a drill-down chain underneath it for anyone who needs to go from that high-level answer down to the exact session and the exact student. A companion tab, "Campus-wise Stats", sits on the same tab strip and answers a related but different question (see Campus Analytics below): this page starts from the subject, that one starts from the campus.

- **Filters** — a date range (so a coordinator can look at a specific term or week rather than all-time), campus, and a subject search box, letting staff narrow a large subject list down quickly.
- **The subject-level table** — Subject, Students, Present, Total sessions, and Attendance %. This is a genuinely aggregated view: "Present" and "Total sessions" are summed across every student and every session for that subject, so the Attendance % shown is a weighted figure (total present divided by total possible), not a simple average of each student's own percentage — a subject with a few students who missed a lot can pull this number down more or less than it would if it were a plain average, depending on how sessions are distributed.
- **Drill-down, level one — Subject → Students.** Clicking a subject row opens the roster of every student enrolled in that one subject, each with their own attendance percentage — this is where a coordinator would go to see, within a subject that's flagged as a problem, exactly which students are driving that number down.
- **Drill-down, level two — Subject → Sessions.** Clicking "Units" on a subject instead opens a session-by-session breakdown: one row per individual class session or curriculum unit that's been held for that subject, each with its own attendance figures.
- **Drill-down, level three — Session → Students.** Clicking into one specific session shows every student's Present/Absent status for that single class, along with how it was marked — the finest level of detail the app exposes, useful for resolving a specific attendance dispute ("I was marked absent on this exact date") down to the actual session record.
- **Search and export at every level** — each of the three levels (subject list, student roster, session list) can be searched independently, and each has its own CSV export, so staff don't have to export the whole subject list just to get one session's attendance sheet.

## Assessments

Assessments is deliberately a separate page from attendance stats, because it tracks a different thing entirely: quiz *completion*, not class attendance. A student can attend every session and still not have finished the associated classroom quiz (CQ) or module quiz (MQ) — this page is where that gap becomes visible. It follows the same Campus → Subject → Student drill-down shape as Student Attendance Stats, which is a deliberate consistency: once staff learn to navigate one drill-down page in this app, the others behave the same way.

- **Filters** — campus and semester, so a coordinator can look at completion for the term that's actually in progress rather than the whole historical record.
- **Summary tiles** — headline CQ%, MQ%, and a combined completion percentage across the current scope, plus two tiles that are visibly greyed out and marked "Coming soon": Skill Assessments and the Final Skill Assessment. These aren't bugs or missing data — they're placeholders for two scoring pillars the product intends to add later; today they show zero because there is genuinely no underlying data for them yet, not because something is broken.
- **Campus-level table** — Students, Subjects, CQ, MQ, Total, CQ%, MQ%, and Overall% per campus, giving a one-screen comparison across every campus in scope.
- **Drill-down to Subject, then Student** — clicking through narrows from campus to a specific subject's completion numbers, and again from subject down to individual students, where a Section column and a direct link to that student's SPI report are added — letting staff go from "which campus is behind on quizzes" all the way down to "which specific student, in which section, hasn't finished theirs."
- **Export at every level** — CSV export is available at the campus, subject, and student levels independently, matching the pattern from Student Attendance Stats.

Worth remembering: because the Skill Assessment and Final Skill Assessment categories carry no real weight in the underlying SPI calculation today, the completion percentages shown here for CQ and MQ are the two components that actually move a student's real SPI score — the two greyed-out tiles are informative about what's planned, not about what's currently counted.

## Campus Analytics

A Campus → Section → Student drill-down based purely on overall attendance percentage — a different cut than Student Attendance Stats, which is subject-based. It's left out of BOA's sidebar since they're already scoped to one campus, though the page itself has no separate access restriction.

Where Student Attendance Stats starts from the subject and asks "which subjects are struggling," Campus Analytics starts from the campus and asks "which sections, within this campus, are struggling" — using the same overall attendance percentage, just sliced along a different axis. The two pages are two views onto attendance data, not two different data sources, so the same underlying facts can be read either way depending on which question a coordinator is trying to answer that day.

- **Filters** — campus and attendance band, plus a "show all students in this campus" checkbox that bypasses the section level entirely for a coordinator who wants the full student list for a campus without navigating through each section first.
- **Drill-down*(* — Campus → Section → Student, each level with its own breadcrumb trail so it's always clear how deep into the hierarchy the current view is, and its own search box for narrowing a long list quickly.
- **Export at every level** — three separate CSV exports (campuses.csv, sections.csv, students.csv) matching whichever level is currently open, so a coordinator preparing a campus-wide report doesn't have to manually stitch together section-level exports.

## Recovery

Recovery is the page where the app moves from *reporting* a problem to actually *doing* something about it. Everywhere else in the dashboard shows percentages; here, a subject falling below the recovery threshold turns into a concrete workflow — schedule a class, run it, report on it, and have that report reflected back everywhere the subject's status is shown. It's also the one area of the Recovery tab that every role can reach, including instructors, because instructors are the ones who actually deliver the recovery classes this page tracks. It has two sub-tabs that look similar but answer different questions and drive different actions.

### Attendance tab

This is the primary recovery workflow: subjects where campus-wide attendance has fallen below the 80% threshold, meaning students there are at risk of not meeting the requirement to progress. Staff pick a campus and semester, and see a card per affected subject. Clicking a subject card opens the **Session Tracker** — the detailed working page for that one subject at that one campus.

The Session Tracker is a table with one row per curriculum topic in that subject, and each row carries everything staff need to act: the topic's regular (non-recovery) attendance percentage, its current recovery status (one of four values — Not Taught, Needs Recovery, Scheduled, or Recovered), and, once a recovery session exists for it, that session's date, the instructor who ran or will run it, whether that instructor was the topic's regular campus instructor or a backup/stand-in, any remarks the instructor left, and a link to the QA report filed for that session.

From the Session Tracker, staff can schedule a brand-new recovery session for a topic that needs one, choosing a date and an instructor. From there, what happens next depends on who's looking at the row:

- **Instructors** see their own assigned sessions and can "Mark complete" one once it's been held — reporting whether it was Completed, Partially Completed, or Not Completed, which specific topics were actually covered in that session, an attendance count for the recovery class itself, free-text remarks about how it went, and — required whenever the outcome is Completed or Partially Completed — a link to the QA report filed for it. This submission is what actually flips the topic's status to Recovered (or leaves it as Needs Recovery, if the report says Not Completed).
- **Admins and Super Admins** have two extra powers instructors don't: they can revert a session that was mistakenly marked Completed or Partially Completed back to "needs recovery" — clearing out its remarks and QA report link in the process, since those no longer describe anything that actually happened — and they can edit an already-scheduled session's instructor-type classification (Campus / Backup / Unknown), useful for auditing whether recovery classes are consistently being run by someone who actually knows that topic. The revert action only appears on rows that are currently marked Recovered, and only to admin-level accounts — an instructor or BOA account never sees that button at all, regardless of which row they're looking at.
- **Anyone with edit rights** can delete a session that's been scheduled but not yet reported on, which simply returns its topics to the "needs recovery" queue as if the session had never been scheduled — useful for correcting a scheduling mistake before any class has actually happened.

### Quiz tab

The second sub-tab tracks a different, narrower problem: subjects where students haven't finished their classroom or module quizzes at 100% completion. Unlike the Attendance tab, this is a **follow-up-only** view — it doesn't schedule lecture-style recovery sessions, because there's no class to re-teach here, just quizzes students still need to finish. Clicking a subject card opens the specific list of students who are still short of 100%, with their individual classroom-quiz and module-quiz completion percentages and averages highlighted, so a coordinator can follow up directly with the named students rather than working from an aggregate percentage.

## Report Recovery (instructor-only page)

Report Recovery is the dedicated landing page (`/instructor`) an instructor sees the moment they log in — not the shared Dashboard everyone else gets, and a deliberately narrower page focused on a single job: running recovery sessions and reporting on them.

- **Session list scoped to campus, not to person.** The page shows the recovery sessions assigned to that instructor's campus for a date they choose. Because many campuses use one shared instructor login (for example `cdu.instructor@nxtwave.co.in`) rather than individual per-person accounts, whoever is signed in with that shared login can act on *any* session scheduled at that campus for that date — the system doesn't try to match a session to one specific named person, only to the campus.
- **Reporting a session.** For each session, the instructor sees which curriculum topics it's meant to cover, a checklist to mark which of those were actually covered, a field for the attendance count during the recovery class itself, a required status choice (Completed / Partially Completed / Not Completed), a free-text remarks field, and a QA report link — required whenever the status is anything other than Not Completed, since a class that was actually delivered is expected to have a quality-assurance record behind it.
- **What submitting actually does.** Submitting this form is the single action that updates the underlying recovery-progress record for each covered topic — and that update is exactly what the Session Tracker on the Recovery → Attendance tab then reflects. In other words, this page and the Session Tracker are two views of the same underlying state: an instructor fills this one in, and admins/BOA see the result show up on that one, with no separate sync step in between.

## Request Inbox

The Request Inbox is a moderation queue — visible only to Super Admin, Admin, BOA, and HOD — for the attendance-correction requests students submit themselves from their own public SPI report page. It exists because a student who believes they were marked absent by mistake needs a channel to say so, and someone on staff needs a place to review and decide those claims rather than fielding them informally over email or in person.

- **What each request shows** — the student, their campus, an overall status (Pending / Verified / Rejected / Partial), how many specific dates are being disputed, and when the request was submitted. A request can cover several disputed dates at once rather than requiring a separate submission per date.
- **Deciding a request** — a request that only disputes a single date can be Verified or Rejected directly from the list view, no extra click needed. A request disputing multiple dates instead opens a detail view where **each date gets its own independent decision** — so a five-date request could end up with three dates verified and two rejected, and the request's overall status becomes "Partial" to reflect that mixed outcome rather than forcing an all-or-nothing call.
- **Staying on top of the queue** — the sidebar shows a live unread-count badge for this inbox, so Admin/BOA/HOD staff can see at a glance whether there's anything waiting without having to open the page speculatively. Two reviewers can't accidentally overwrite each other's decision on the same request at the same moment — if one person's decision is already in when another tries to submit a stale one, the second submission is rejected and that person is prompted to refresh and see the current state first.

## Data Explorer

The Data Explorer is Super Admin only, and reached deliberately from the profile menu rather than the main sidebar — a signal that it's a different kind of tool from the rest of the app: a raw, read-only browser directly over the three BigQuery tables that power every percentage shown anywhere else in the dashboard (session-wise attendance detail, classroom/module quiz detail, and the scheduled-lecture "prod sequence" table). Where every other page in the app pre-shapes the data into a specific question ("which subjects are below 80%"), this page lets a Super Admin ask an arbitrary question directly against the source tables — useful for investigating a number that looks wrong elsewhere, or for one-off analysis that doesn't map to any existing page.

- **Table list view** — every accessible table with its column names and types, plus a search that works across tables, so a Super Admin can find which table holds a given field without already knowing the schema by heart.
- **Table data view** — free-text search, per-column filters (a dropdown for columns with a manageable number of distinct values, a "contains" text filter for everything else), paginated raw rows, and a CSV export capped at 10,000 rows. That cap is enforced on both the preview and the export path, and — like the exports elsewhere in the app — a query matching more than 10,000 rows will simply return the first 10,000 with no explicit warning that more rows exist beyond the cap.

Because this page is a direct window onto the warehouse rather than a curated view, it's the one place in the app where someone could, in principle, see data that doesn't map cleanly onto any of the friendlier pages elsewhere — which is exactly why it's restricted to Super Admin alone.

## Profile

Profile is the one page every single role — including instructors, who otherwise have almost no access to the rest of the app — can reach. It's deliberately minimal: this is account self-service, not account administration.

- **Edit full name.** Email and role are shown on the page for reference but are read-only — changing either of those requires an admin to do it from User Access, since they affect what the account can see and do, not just how it's labeled.
- **Change password.** Requires entering the current password correctly first, and a new password of at least 6 characters. Changing a password here has one consequence worth knowing: it signs the account out of every *other* device or browser session currently logged in (a security measure, in case the password was changed because it was compromised elsewhere), while keeping the current session — the one used to make the change — logged in, so the person isn't locked out of the device they're actively using.

## User Access (Admin)

User Access is where every staff login in the system gets created, scoped, and retired — restricted to Super Admin and Admin. This page is the practical mechanism behind the role-based scoping described throughout this guide: an account's campuses and subjects are set here, and that's what determines what that person will be able to see everywhere else in the app the next time they log in.

- **The staff table** — name and email, role (shown with a short description of what that role can do, so an admin doesn't have to remember the difference between HOD and Capability Manager from memory), scope (the specific campuses/subjects assigned, or "All access" for roles that see everything), active status, and last login — useful for spotting accounts that have gone dormant.
- **Creating a user** — a panel for name, email, password, role, and — shown only for the roles that need it (Capability Manager, BOA, Instructor) — which specific campuses and subjects that account should be scoped to. Roles like Super Admin, Admin, and HOD don't get this scoping section at all, since those roles see everything by definition.
- **Bulk import** — a tool for creating many logins at once from a list, rather than clicking through the single-user form repeatedly. This is how campuses set up the "one shared instructor login per campus" pattern mentioned elsewhere in this guide (for example, generating `cdu.instructor@nxtwave.co.in` for CDU campus) in one pass instead of one at a time, and it's built to tolerate a messy input list — rows with problems (a bad email format, a duplicate that already exists, an invalid role) are skipped or flagged individually rather than causing the whole batch to fail.
- **Editing and permissions on this page itself** — an Admin can edit or deactivate the accounts of instructors, BOA, HOD, and Capability Managers, but cannot touch another Admin's account or a Super Admin's account — only a Super Admin can edit or deactivate accounts at that level. This prevents one Admin from locking out or downgrading another Admin.

## Campus Setup (Admin)

Campus Setup maintains the canonical list of campuses that every other page in the app relies on for filtering and scoping — also Super Admin/Admin only. It's a small page, but an important one to get right, because it's the single source of truth every campus filter, every scoped-user assignment, and every campus-level report ultimately reads from.

- **The campus table** — campus name, an institute ID, and the date the campus was added to the system.
- **Add, edit, or delete a campus.** The one detail that matters most here: **a campus's name in this table must match the institute name used in the underlying BigQuery data exactly.** If it doesn't, scope filtering for that campus silently stops working correctly — a BOA or Capability Manager assigned to a mismatched campus name could see no data, or the wrong data, for their own campus, with no error message pointing at the actual cause. Renaming a campus here also does not automatically ripple through to update the `campuses` list already stored on existing user accounts that reference the old name — those accounts would need to be re-scoped manually if a campus is ever renamed.

## Public-facing pages

Three pages are reachable without any staff login at all — the parts of the app students and the public actually see:

- **Landing page** (`/`) — a marketing/explainer page aimed at students, laying out what the SPI score is, how it's currently weighted (Classroom Quizzes 10%, Module Quizzes 15%, Skill Assessments 25%, Final Skill Assessment 50%, per the page's own displayed breakdown), and what the Healthy / Recovery / Critical standing bands mean. Worth knowing: this displayed weighting is the *aspirational* breakdown the product is working toward, not necessarily an exact mirror of every number currently driving a student's live score today — Skill Assessments and the Final Skill Assessment are still "Coming soon" placeholders elsewhere in the app, so those two categories don't yet contribute real data to what a student's actual SPI score reflects.
- **Staff Login** (`/staff-login`) — the entry point for every staff role. Supports plain email/password sign-in as well as Google Workspace SSO for anyone whose organization email is already provisioned as an account in User Access. SSO does not create new accounts on its own — an admin has to have already created that person's login first; SSO only handles authenticating into an account that already exists.
- **SPI Report** (`/spi/:studentId`) — a student's own shareable, always-live report, and the single most important public page in the app. It shows the student's overall SPI score with a gauge visualization, a per-category breakdown (again including the "coming soon" Skill Assessment placeholders), course-wise attendance cards for each subject they're enrolled in, and — whenever any one course drops below the 80% requirement — a "Recovery Mode" banner explaining the consequence and what the student needs to do about it. This is also where the **Request Attendance** flow lives: a student who believes an attendance mark is wrong can submit a correction request directly from this page, listing the specific disputed dates and a reason, and that submission is exactly what appears in staff's Request Inbox for review. Because this page works from a link rather than a login, anyone holding that link can view it — the link itself is what grants access, so it should be treated similarly to any other personal, unlisted link.

## A few things worth knowing

- The **"Recovery instructors" admin tab** (which linked historical instructor names, imported as plain text from older reports, to real staff accounts) was removed on Sep 16, 2026. It's no longer in the app in any form — the page, its route, and its backend endpoints were all deleted — because campuses have moved to the one-shared-instructor-login-per-campus pattern instead, which makes that manual name-matching step unnecessary.
- **Skill Assessments** and the **Final Skill Assessment** appear as locked "Coming soon" tiles on both the Assessments tab and the student SPI report. These are planned scoring pillars that simply don't exist as data yet — not a bug, and not something currently factored into anyone's real score, however they may be described elsewhere.
- Nearly every list-style page in this app follows the same underlying pattern: search box, a set of filters, a sortable and paginated table, a CSV export, and a click-through drill-down with its own breadcrumb trail back up. That consistency is deliberate — once one of these pages feels familiar, most of the others in the app will already feel familiar too, even before reading about them specifically.
- For a deeper, more technical look at exactly how the numbers on these pages are calculated — the underlying data model, the SPI formula, the attendance and recovery thresholds, caching behavior, and a few known rough edges worth being aware of — see the companion **"How It Works"** tab on this same document.
